Face tracking lets a frame pin widgets to your face instead of a fixed spot: Hope over your head, a nameplate under your chin. It is **off by default**, and every frame works without it.

## What it does and doesn't do

- It finds a box around your face and six points. That's all.
- It doesn't read expressions or recognise who you are.
- It never changes your stats. It only moves widgets.
- It runs on your machine. Camera frames are never stored or sent.

The detector is about 230 KB and is served from vellumhud.com along with the app.

## Turning it on

With tracking on, Vellum opens your webcam itself instead of OBS's webcam source, so the HUD and your face stay perfectly in sync.

1. **Start OBS with camera access for browser sources.** Add `--enable-media-stream` to how you launch OBS:
   - **Windows:** edit the OBS shortcut's **Target** and add ` --enable-media-stream` after the closing quote.
   - **macOS:** run `open -a OBS --args --enable-media-stream` in Terminal.
   - **Linux:** run `obs --enable-media-stream`, or add it to the `Exec=` line of your OBS desktop file.
2. **Switch to the "Vellum camera" scene.** It contains only the Vellum source, and Vellum draws your webcam underneath the HUD.
3. **Pick a frame that uses face anchors.** The picker marks them.

If the camera won't open, the setup panel tells you why and shows the fix for your system. On Windows, close anything else using the webcam, including OBS's own Video Capture Device.

## For frame makers

A widget can anchor to `face-top`, `face-bottom`, `face-left` or `face-right`. Every face anchor needs a `fallback` fixed anchor, used when tracking is off or your face leaves the frame. Names may change before release; the frame schema will be the final word.
