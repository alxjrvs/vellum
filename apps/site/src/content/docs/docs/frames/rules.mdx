---
title: The two rules
description: Every Vellum frame keeps labels readable and reads outside-in. Here's exactly what that means.
---

A frame can change colors, positions, sizes, and bars or chits. It can't break these two rules. Vellum checks the measurable parts automatically.

## 1. Labels are always readable

People see your HUD as a small tile in a call, often 640 × 360 or smaller.

- **Size:** at least 2.1% of the frame's width. That's **40 px** on the 1920 canvas, about 13 px on a 640 × 360 tile. Values are bigger still (64 px).
- **Contrast:** **7:1 or better** against the plate behind the label.
- **Backing:** a label always sits on a solid plate, never straight on video.
- **Dimming:** inactive things dim by color, never by transparency.

## 2. Everything reads outside-in

Each widget reads from the nearest frame edge toward your face, so the HUD frames you instead of crowding you.

- **Left side:** label, value, track.
- **Right side, mirrored:** track, value, label.
- **Accent edges and slants** sit on the outer edge too.
- Widgets at top center and bottom center read left to right as normal.
- In the frame creator, a widget mirrors once its center crosses the middle of the stage.

## Why these two

Labels that can't be read on a small tile are decoration. And a HUD that reads inward toward the face keeps every player's tile looking like a broadcast lower-third instead of a spreadsheet.
