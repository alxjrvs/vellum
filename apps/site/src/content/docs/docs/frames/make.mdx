## With the frame creator

The creator opens full screen from the frame picker on the app's setup screen. Its stage is the real HUD, so what you see is what goes on camera.

1. **Start from a frame.** Pick any frame, then choose **Make a frame from this one**. Your copy goes on the **My frames** shelf.
2. **Drag widgets into place.** Drag the stats, conditions, dice and nameplate anywhere on the stage, or select one and nudge it with the arrow keys (hold Shift for bigger steps). Widgets snap to a grid and stop when they meet another widget, so nothing overlaps; the ticker and Setup button areas are kept clear too. A widget past the middle of the stage mirrors so its labels read outside-in. **Reset layout** puts everything back.
3. **Choose bar or chits** for each stat.
4. **Set the colors.** The creator lists anything that breaks [the two rules](/docs/frames/rules/) as you go, and won't save until it's fixed.
5. **Check the stage.** The preview is the whole 1920 × 1080 camera frame, scaled down.
6. **Save or export.** Saved frames stay in your browser. **Export file** writes a `.vellum-frame.json` you can share, import on another machine, or [submit to the Library](/docs/frames/submit/).

When you copy your OBS link while wearing one of your frames, the frame travels inside the link, so OBS can wear it without a server.

Changing widget sizes is planned.

## By hand

A frame is a `ThemeConfig`. Export one from the creator, or copy `src/themes/daggerheart.theme.ts`, change the colors, the `tracks` map (`'bar'` or `'chits'` per stat) and the optional `regions` map (where each widget sits: a named spot such as `'top-left'`, or `{ x, y }` for the widget's center and top edge in stage pixels), then run `bun run test`. The frame rules test fails if a label drops below 40 px or 7:1 contrast, or if two widgets share a named spot. Hand-placed widgets are checked for overlap in the creator, so open your frame there before you submit it.
